The Insulation Guide

Insulation does not make heat; it traps the heat your body makes. Here is how down and synthetic fills do that, and what the numbers on the label mean.

How insulation works

Your body warms a thin layer of air next to the skin. Insulation holds that air still so it cannot drift away. The more still air a jacket traps, the warmer it is, which is why thickness, or loft, matters more than weight.

Down and fill power

Down is the soft under-plumage of ducks and geese. Fill power measures how much space one ounce fills: higher numbers mean more loft per gram.

Fill powerQualityTypical use
500–600GoodCity and everyday jackets
600–700Very goodAll-round winter jackets
700–800ExcellentLightweight warm jackets
800+PremiumUltralight and expedition gear

Fill weight: the other half

Fill power tells you quality; fill weight tells you quantity. A jacket with 200 g of 650-fill down will usually be warmer than one with 100 g of 850-fill. When both numbers are listed, look at them together.

Synthetic insulation

Synthetic fills are made of fine polyester fibres, either in sheets or loose clusters that imitate down. They keep more of their warmth when wet and dry quickly, which suits damp weather, sweaty activity and anyone who prefers to avoid animal products. They are a little heavier and bulkier for the same warmth.

Construction matters too

  • Sewn-through baffles: light and simple, but cold spots along the stitching.
  • Box baffles: fabric walls between chambers; warmer, used in heavier jackets.
  • Hybrid: insulation in the core with stretch fleece under the arms for movement.

Choosing the right warmth

Think about your coldest regular day and how active you will be. Standing still at a bus stop needs much more insulation than a brisk walk. The Warmth Picker suggests a starting point.

Stay warm. Stay dry. Choose well.
