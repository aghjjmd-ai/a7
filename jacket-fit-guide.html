The Jacket Fit Guide

A warm jacket only works if it fits: too tight and the insulation is squashed flat, too loose and cold air pumps in with every step.

Measure yourself

  1. Chest: around the fullest part, under the arms, tape level.
  2. Sleeve: from the centre back of the neck, over the shoulder, to the wrist bone.
  3. Back length: from the base of the neck to where you want the hem.

Measure over the kind of layer you normally wear under a coat.

Room for layers

Insulation needs space to loft. Allow roughly 4 cm extra at the chest for a jumper and 8 cm for a thick knit. If the jacket feels tight over your normal layers, the down is compressed and you lose warmth.

Length by style

  • Short puffer: ends at the hip; free movement, less warmth below.
  • Mid-length: covers the seat; the best everyday balance.
  • Parka: mid-thigh or longer; maximum warmth for standing still.

Sleeves, cuffs and hood

Sleeves should cover your wrist bones when you reach forward. Adjustable cuffs seal out wind. A hood should turn with your head; try looking over your shoulder with it up.

Common fit problems

  • Pulling across the back: size up or choose a regular cut.
  • Sleeves too short: look for tall sizes or raglan sleeves.
  • Gaping collar: a draw-cord or adjustable collar helps.
  • Hem rides up: a longer back panel or drop hem fixes this.

Stay warm. Stay dry. Choose well.
